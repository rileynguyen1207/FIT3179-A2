Australian nut industry | 2024–25

FROM AUSTRALIAN ORCHARDS TO THE WORLD

How geography shapes what Australia grows — and where its nuts go.

Australia's nut industry is shaped by more than what grows in its orchards. Different crops thrive in different regions, contribute very different amounts to the economy, and reach different markets overseas. Follow seven Australian nuts from their growing regions to their economic and export profiles.

01

Where it begins

Australia's nut industry begins in very different landscapes.

From tropical Queensland to southern Australia's temperate growing regions, the geography of nut production varies substantially by crop.

Chart 01 / Growing regions

Australia's nut-growing regions cluster along the south and east

Almond, pistachio and walnut growing regions concentrate in the south, while macadamia regions extend north along the warmer east coast.

The geography behind the crops

  • Macadamia growing regions extend furthest north of any nut type, reaching into tropical Queensland, while every other crop stays within the cooler southern states.
  • The dataset lists 10 representative chestnut growing regions across 5 states — more locations than any other nut — compared with a single listed region for pecan.
  • Southern NSW and Victoria host the greatest overlap of growing regions, with most nut types represented across both states.

Explore the map Click a nut type in the legend to highlight its representative growing regions.

Points represent growing regions, not individual orchards. Some nearby points have been slightly displaced to improve visibility.

Sources: Nuts for Life; Australian Macadamias; ABS ASGS Edition 3.

Data & methodology

Growing regions are based on published descriptions of Australian nut-growing areas. Map coordinates represent approximate locations within those regions and should not be interpreted as precise farm locations.

Where multiple nut types share the same representative coordinates, small visual offsets are applied to prevent markers from overlapping. These offsets have no geographic meaning.

All symbols use the same size because the source does not provide a consistent measure of regional production importance across nut types.

Most Australian nut crops are concentrated in a few states

Pecan, macadamia and chestnut show particularly strong single-state concentration, while almond and pistachio production is distributed more broadly.

Where production is concentrated

02

What Australia grows

Seven crops. One overwhelmingly dominant industry leader.

Australian nut crops differ dramatically in economic scale, with almond production accounting for most of the industry's reported value.

Chart 04 / Production value

Almond dominates the value of Australia's nut industry

At $1,327.9 million in 2025, almond production was worth more than seven times macadamia and accounted for about 80% of the industry's reported value.

A one-crop industry, by value

  • Almond production alone was worth more than the other six displayed nut types combined.
  • Macadamia ranked second at $181.8 million, more than five times the value of pecan or walnut.
  • Pecan, walnut and pistachio were close together, each worth approximately $33–36 million.

03

An industry in motion

The industry grew. Not every nut moved with it.

Australia's nut production value reached a five-year high in 2025, but changes across individual crops were far from equal.

Chart 05 / Five-year trend

Industry production value dipped sharply in 2023, then rebounded to a five-year high in 2025

Industry-wide production value fell by about 40% from 2022 to 2023 before rebounding to $1,655.7 million in 2025 — about 39% above its 2021 level.

A sharp dip, then recovery

  • The industry's reported production value fell from $1,229.8 million in 2022 to $742.6 million in 2023.
  • Production value then nearly doubled between 2023 and 2024.
  • By 2025, the industry reached its highest reported production value within the five-year period.

04

Leaving Australia

Australian nuts travel far — but their markets are far from equal.

The latest available country-level export data reveals major differences in where Australian nuts are sold overseas.

Chart 08 / Global export connections

China appears repeatedly among Australia's major reported nut export destinations

Destination patterns vary by crop, with China featuring prominently in several reported export markets.

Global export patterns

  • China is the top reported destination for four of the seven nut types: almond, macadamia, pistachio and hazelnut.
  • Market concentration varies widely by crop — pistachio and hazelnut send the large majority of their reported exports to China, while pecan's exports split across New Zealand, Belgium and the United States.
  • Walnut is the most geographically spread crop, with India, Germany, Turkey, France and Belgium all receiving meaningful reported volumes.

How to explore Use the dropdown above the map to show one nut type's reported destinations, or keep "All nuts (aggregate)" selected to see every connection together.

Source: Hort Innovation Australian Horticulture Statistics Handbook 2024–25. World boundaries: Natural Earth / FIT3179 teaching resources.

Data & methodology

The map combines the latest available country-level destination breakdown for each nut type. Almond and Macadamia use 2024 data because their 2025 country-level breakdowns are not fully reported in the source; other nuts use 2025 data.

The all-nuts view aggregates reported export tonnes across different nut types and measurement bases (mostly Kernel Weight Equivalent; Walnut and Pistachio use a different or unstated basis, together under 6% of the total shown). It should therefore be read as an approximate indication of the geographic distribution of reported exports, not an exact, standardised national export total.

Export volume is represented using a square-root line-width scale to keep smaller flows visible. Destination points represent approximate country locations; lines illustrate connections between Australia and destination countries, not shipping routes, transport modes or ports.

The "Other" category (14.6% of reported export tonnage) is excluded because no specific country is identified in the source; percentages therefore refer only to named destinations. Destinations reported as "<1 tonne" are displayed with a minimum visible line width — their exact quantity is unknown and is not assumed to equal one tonne.

Different nuts, different markets

One export industry. Very different levels of market dependence.

Some nuts rely heavily on a single destination, while others distribute their exports across several markets.

Chart 10 / Export concentration

Some nuts depend heavily on one destination — others have more diversified export profiles

Pistachio and hazelnut rely on a single market for the large majority of their exports, while walnut and pecan spread more evenly across several.

Market concentration

  • Pistachio (92%) and hazelnut (94%) send the large majority of their reported exports to a single country, China.
  • Walnut and pecan are the most diversified crops — no single destination takes more than a third of either one's exports.
  • This shows destination concentration, not observed business risk — a concentrated market is not necessarily a risk that has materialised.

How to read Each treemap shows 100% of one nut’s exports. Equal-sized panels do not mean equal export volumes.

Data & methodology

Each treemap is independently normalised to 100% of the corresponding nut's reported country-level export breakdown. Panel areas are not proportional to total export volume across nut types — Almond's panel and Chestnut's panel are drawn the same size even though Almond exports almost 300,000× more tonnage.

Very small destination shares may be shown without labels to prevent text overlap. Quantities reported as less than one tonne use a small display-only proxy area to remain visible; tooltips and labels always show "<1 t", never a fabricated number.

Chestnut is a special case: its only reported destination has an unspecified quantity below one tonne, so although the box fills 100% of the panel, this reflects a single negligible shipment, not an established trade relationship.

06

One industry, many profiles

There is no single Australian nut industry profile.

Economic scale, export intensity, growth and domestic supply vary across crops — and no single measure tells the whole story.

Chart 11 / Industry profile comparison

Almond leads on scale and domestic supply, but Macadamia is proportionally more export-intensive

Each axis is independently scaled from the lowest (0) to the highest (100) of the seven nuts — a relative index, not a percentage of the metric itself.

Four profiles, one industry

  • Macadamia's export value is 136% of its own production value — the highest ratio of any nut, reflecting how the two figures are measured rather than more than 100% of output being shipped overseas.
  • Pecan and Hazelnut have the lowest export intensity, each around 7% — most of their production stays in the domestic market.
  • Almond leads on per-capita domestic supply at 0.91 kg per person, more than three times the next-highest nut (Walnut, 0.28 kg).

How to explore Click a nut in the legend to isolate it, or shift-click to compare a different pair.

Source: Hort Innovation Australian Horticulture Statistics Handbook 2024–25.

Data & methodology

The radar compares four indicators derived from the latest national nut summary: production value (A$m), export value intensity, year-on-year production-value growth (2025 vs 2024), and per-capita domestic supply (kg per person).

Export value intensity is calculated as reported export value divided by production value. It indicates a relationship between two monetary values, not the physical proportion of production exported — the ratio can exceed 100% since the two figures can be reported on different value bases.

Each indicator is independently normalised across the seven nut types on a 0–100 relative scale (highest = 100, lowest = 0). Because each axis uses its own scale, the radar compares relative positions rather than absolute magnitudes or percentages.

Export value is not reported in the source for Chestnut. Its export value intensity is therefore missing, not zero, and the corresponding radar vertex is omitted rather than plotted at the centre — select Chestnut in the legend to see this noted directly on the chart.

Three things to take away

  1. 01Geography shapes production.

    Australia's nut crops have distinct growing regions and different patterns of state concentration. NSW and Victoria each lead three of the seven nut types, while Queensland leads macadamia.

  2. 02Almond sets the economic scale.

    Almond accounted for approximately 80% of Australia's reported nut production value in 2025 and recorded the largest absolute increase from 2024.

  3. 03Export markets are not equally diversified.

    China dominates the reported destinations for several nuts, particularly hazelnut and pistachio. Walnut and pecan show more distributed leading markets.

Australia's nut industry is not one uniform story. Its geography, economic value and export relationships change considerably from crop to crop.

Data & methodology

Nuts are measured on different bases depending on the crop and how the source reports it — e.g. almond, pecan and hazelnut on a Kernel Weight Equivalent basis; macadamia, walnut and pistachio In-Shell. Charts that compare volumes across nut types note this directly, and any total that mixes bases is flagged as an approximation rather than an exact sum.

Each chart uses the most recent year the handbook reports for that nut; where a 2025 breakdown was not yet published (e.g. Almond and Macadamia's export destinations, which use 2024 figures), the chart note beneath it says so.
