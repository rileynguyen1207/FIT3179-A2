Chart 11 / Parallel coordinates

The largest crop is not the fastest-growing crop

Follow each coloured line across three measures. Domestic supply per person is the reported amount available in Australia divided by the population; it is not measured individual consumption. The default comparison highlights almond, macadamia and pecan.

Supply-axis caution: kg/person uses different weight bases: almond, macadamia, pecan, hazelnut and chestnut use kernel weight equivalent; walnut combines kernel and in-shell quantities; pistachio’s basis is unspecified. These values are not directly comparable edible quantities.

Key takeaway

Scale, growth and domestic supply tell different stories.

Almond leads on production value; pecan grew faster in percentage terms. Macadamia’s production value fell. Supply figures retain each crop’s reported weight basis.

Reading the chart

Each axis has its own units. Production value uses a log scale: equal steps mean tenfold differences. Growth shows actual percentages, including negatives; 0% means no change. Hover over a point for all three values.

Source: Hort Innovation, Australian Horticulture Statistics Handbook 2024–25. Source details

Data & methodology

Production value and domestic supply per person come from the 2025 national summary. Growth is calculated from 2024 and 2025 production values, consistently with the heatmap.

The monetary axis uses a logarithmic scale to keep both large and small crops visible. Growth and domestic supply per person use linear scales. Connecting segments show crop profiles, not trends between time points.

All seven crops remain in the chart. The selector changes emphasis; it does not change the axes. No 0–100 performance score or export-intensity ratio is used.
